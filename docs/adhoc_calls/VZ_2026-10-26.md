## How I think the crowd digests it (my own read; H unless marked)
- Verizon's crowd has had a clean story for a year: a new CEO, net adds back, churn down, guidance raised twice, and the stock bought on every print since the 2024 subscriber-loss quarters. Into that story a new entrant landed twice in October, and the sector was de-rated on a threat that is still a plan. That is the whole question today: does a third raise and another quarter of net adds buy back some of the SpaceX discount, or does the crowd treat the numbers as backward-looking against a forward threat?
- I lean to the first, for three reasons. Verizon fell least on Friday, which says the crowd already separates it from the two carriers with more to lose; the print is the first chance for management to answer the fourth-carrier question with numbers (spectrum, fiber via Frontier, churn at 0.84 %); and the name's own eight-print record is the most reliable up-habit in the ledger, the mirror of AmEx's down-habit, which I respected on Friday and which paid.
- Against it: a telecom beat is a small beat (EPS +2-5 %), the service-revenue line is a +2.8 % number in a tape that sells anything priced for growth, and any promo or price-response language to SpaceX reads as the price-war crowd's confirmation. The 2024 prints (−5 to −6 %) show what a net-adds miss does to this name.
- Net read: adjusted EPS ~1.30, revenue ~$34.9 B, service revenue +3 %+, postpaid phone net adds 150-250k, churn ≤ 0.85 %, FY EPS guide nudged to ~$5.02-5.07 or FCF raised; gap +1 to +2 %, close +2 to +4 %. P(up) 0.58.
